\paragraph{The failure.}
This section and the next use the frozen noise-free evaluation set of \pv{ra.evaln} problems, with every adapter evaluated as
trained (Section~\ref{sec:methods}).
With sequence-level credit, the tokens of $\sigma$ share the answer's advantage, and the proper scoring rule did not
keep the stated $\sigma$ honest.
Trained beyond the matched step to step \pv{ra.collapse.step}, the uniform-credit policy states $\sigma = 0$ on
\pv{ra.collapse.zero} of the evaluation answers, and its $1\sigma$ coverage falls to \pv{ra.collapse.cov}.
The score does not favour this, since it is proper; the collapse is a property of the optimization.
At the matched step (\pv{grpo.step}) it is partial: across the three uniform-credit seeds, $\sigma = 0$ on
\pv{ra.zero.uniform} of answers and the median relative $\sigma$ is \pv{ra.sigma.uniform}.
The warm-up model's own $\sigma$ is as narrow: a single scale factor fitted on the calibration split multiplies it by
\pv{ra.posthoc.fit} to maximize the score, and would need \pv{ra.posthoc.cov} to reach nominal $1\sigma$ coverage
there.

% FINDINGS #16 (family S, pre-registered, D62's seed-mean rule), #18 (3 vs 3 seeds, D71: a robustness analysis, not
% the verdict); results/corrected/robustness_3v3/report.json; Figure headline A.
\paragraph{Split credit fixes coverage.}
Split credit gives the tokens of $\sigma$ an advantage of their own (Section~\ref{sec:grpo}).
With \pv{split.seeds} seeds per arm, it raises mean $1\sigma$ coverage from \pv{ra.cov.uniform} under uniform credit to
\pv{ra.cov.split}, a difference of \pv{ra.cov.diff} (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}A); the warm-up model alone covers
\pv{ra.cov.sft}.
Every split seed beats every uniform seed (\pv{ra.cov.pairs} cross-seed pairs; \pv{ra.cov.split.seeds} against
\pv{ra.cov.uniform.seeds}).
The collapse disappears: split credit states $\sigma = 0$ on \pv{ra.zero.split} of answers, with a median relative
$\sigma$ of \pv{ra.sigma.split}.
Accuracy does not change (\pv{ra.acc.split} against \pv{ra.acc.uniform}), so the pre-registered tests S1a, S1b and S3
are supported.
Coverage nonetheless stays far below the nominal \pv{gate.nominal.one}.

% FINDINGS #16 (S2 not supported; family N fails on every seed), #18 (rho: 9 / 9 cross-seed pairs, not a seed-level
% significance claim, R2-4); D49, D50/A5 (RL calibration is judged against the text baselines on the same answers).
\paragraph{It does not fix ranking.}
The wider $\sigma$ says almost nothing about which answers are wrong.
Its Spearman correlation with the error is \pv{ra.rho.split} against uniform credit's \pv{ra.rho.uniform}, a
difference of \pv{ra.rho.diff} with one-sided $p = \pv{ra.rho.p}$, so S2 is not supported.
Every split seed's correlation (\pv{ra.rho.split.seeds}) exceeds every uniform seed's (\pv{ra.rho.pairs} cross-seed
pairs), but the values are tiny, and three seeds per arm cannot make this a seed-level significance claim.
We judge RL calibration against text baselines (A5) that read the problem, the answer and its reasoning and predict
the error of the same answers.
Their $\sigma$ correlates with the error at \pv{ra.rho.afive} per seed, and split credit's $\sigma$ loses
\pv{ra.nfail} pre-registered comparisons with it (family N: coverage error, correlation and score, per seed).

% D52, FINDINGS #14, #16 (E1, corrected); D94, D101, FINDINGS #25 (the E1c / E1d pilots, one seed each); R5-7 (E1d is
% exploratory and scoped to the frozen-answer setting); results/e1/pilots/pilots.json; FINDINGS #20 (X3,

\paragraph{Learning $\sigma$ with the answer fixed (exploratory).}
E1 takes credit assignment out of the question: the answer is fixed and only the $\sigma$ suffix is sampled
(Section~\ref{sec:grpo}).
At learning rate \pv{eone.lr}, E1 widens $\sigma$ ($1\sigma$ coverage \pv{ra.eone.cov}), but its correlation with the
error stays at \pv{ra.eone.rho}; scaling the advantages by the batch instead of the group standard deviation (E1c)
leaves it at \pv{ra.eonec.rho}.
At \pv{eone.lrd} (E1d) the correlation rises to \pv{ra.eoned.rho}, \pv{ra.eoned.diff} above E1's, the level that a
supervised student of the A5 text baseline reaches on the same base and adapter (X3, \pv{ra.xthree.rho}).
E1d's $\sigma$ stays narrow (median \pv{ra.eoned.sigma}, $1\sigma$ coverage \pv{ra.eoned.cov}), and its mean
$\crpslog$, though above that of the warm-up model's $\sigma$ (\pv{ra.eoned.sft}), stays below the A5 baseline's
(\pv{ra.eoned.afive}).
We treat this as exploratory and scoped to the frozen-answer setting: it is a single-seed pilot on noise-free
problems, the suffix is at most \pv{eone.suffix} tokens, and the learning rate is untested on whole completions, where
the answer moves too.
Its correlation also ranks problems, not answers: within a problem, E1d's $\sigma$ is at chance
(Section~\ref{sec:act2}).

\section{Within-problem correctness signals on the fine-tuned model}
\label{sec:act2}
% R6-1 (D166): the claim, scoped to the evaluated checkpoint and its elicitation conditions; FINDINGS #19, #22, #37.
On the fine-tuned \pv{model.base} model and its terse response format, the tested single-completion uncertainty
signals provide little within-problem discrimination between correct and incorrect answers; sample agreement provides
stronger discrimination.
This is specific to the evaluated checkpoint and elicitation conditions and does not establish that small language
models generally lack accessible answer-correctness information.

% D74 (X2), FINDINGS #19; results/x2/results.json; physcal/eval/x2_pairs.py (pairwise accuracy, ties 0.5); R3-7, R5-8
% (CIs are bootstrapped over problems, never over pairs).
\paragraph{Holding difficulty fixed.}
A correlation between $\sigma$ and the error across problems can come from difficulty alone: a readout that knows
which problems are hard ranks them without knowing whether a given answer is right.
To hold difficulty fixed, we sample $k = \pv{rb.k}$ answers from the warm-up model for each of \pv{rb.candidates}
problems and keep the \pv{rb.problems} problems that have both right and wrong answers, \pv{rb.pairs} right--wrong
pairs in all.
A readout's pairwise accuracy on a problem is the share of its pairs in which the right answer gets more confidence
(ties count half); we average it over problems and bootstrap \pv{rb.cilevel} intervals over problems.
Chance is one half.

% FINDINGS #19 (X2: stated sigma, A5, the probe and its random-init control), #22 (length; D92, post-hoc), #26 (E1d
% and X3 within problems; D102); results/x2/robustness/results.json, results/e1d_x2/results.json; Table cost;
% Figure headline B.
\paragraph{Single completions.}
Every readout of a single completion's own uncertainty lies between \pv{rb.single.span}
(Figure~\ref{fig:headline}B).
The warm-up model's stated $\sigma$ scores \pv{rb.acc.sftsigma} and split credit's \pv{rb.acc.splitsigma}; the A5
text baseline's probability of correctness \pv{rb.acc.afivep} and its $\sigma$ \pv{rb.acc.afivesigma}; E1d's RL
$\sigma$ suffix \pv{rb.acc.eoned} and X3's supervised suffix \pv{rb.acc.xthreea}.
E1d and X3 agree to the third decimal: RL with clean credit and supervised distillation of the text baseline land in
the same place.
Answer length alone reaches \pv{rb.acc.length}.
A linear probe on the hidden state at the ``$\pm$'' token reaches \pv{rb.acc.probe}, but the same probe on a randomly
initialized copy of the architecture, reading the same tokens, reaches \pv{rb.acc.randominitprobe}.
The difference, \pv{rb.diff.probe.random}, does not exclude zero, so the probe does not show that the model represents
its own error; nor is it distinguishable from length (\pv{rb.diff.length.probe}).

% D89, D91 (X4), FINDINGS #21; results/x4/results.json.
\paragraph{Self-verification.}
Asked to check its own answer, the model writes a verdict on \pv{rb.xfour.verdict} of answers, and the verdict is
``yes'' on \pv{rb.xfour.yes.correct} of the correct answers and \pv{rb.xfour.yes.incorrect} of the incorrect ones.
Its renormalized probability of ``yes'' is slightly higher on the incorrect answers (\pv{rb.xfour.pyes.incorrect}
against \pv{rb.xfour.pyes.correct}).
Within problems it reaches \pv{rb.acc.xfoursft}, which is not Holm-supported (one-sided $p = \pv{rb.xfour.p}$ against
a threshold of \pv{rb.xfour.threshold}).
The check is short (a median of \pv{rb.xfour.tokens} generated tokens) and behaves like a test of whether the final
line matches the steps: multiplying the final value by \pv{rb.xfour.factor} lowers $P(\text{yes})$ on right and wrong
answers alike, and the ranking does not move (\pv{rb.xfour.perturbed}).
Across problems it is anti-informative, with an AUROC of \pv{rb.xfour.auroc} for correctness.

% FINDINGS #22 (self-consistency; D92, post-hoc exploratory; configs/x2_robustness.yaml), #26 (E1d against it), #37
% (the cost, R5-8; results/r5_diag/cost.json); Tables cost, costmca.
\paragraph{Sample agreement, at a cost.}
Scoring each answer by the share of the problem's other samples that agree with it within \pv{rb.agree} gives the one
strong within-problem signal: \pv{rb.acc.selfconsistency}, \pv{rb.diff.sc.probe} above the probe; E1d's difference
from it is \pv{rb.diff.eoned.sc}.
It needs the other samples.
At \pv{rb.tokens.k} generated tokens per problem against \pv{rb.tokens.one} for one answer, it costs \pv{rb.cost.ratio}
times the tokens (\pv{rb.seconds.k} against \pv{rb.seconds.one} GPU-seconds per problem; Table~\ref{tab:cost}), and the
cost grows linearly with $k$.
The same trade-off appears on A6c for MC-A's spread over re-asked, perturbed copies (Section~\ref{sec:act3}): at
$k = \pv{rc.mca.k}$ its AUROC for a wrong answer is \pv{rc.mca.auroc}, against \pv{rc.mca.free} for the best signal
that needs no extra generation, at \pv{rc.mca.tokens} extra tokens per problem (Table~\ref{tab:costmca}).

% FINDINGS #20 (X3), #25 (E1d on the evaluation set), #26 (across the X2 answers): between-problem ranking is
% difficulty prediction.
\paragraph{Across problems is a different question.}
Across all the sampled answers, E1d's and X3's $\sigma$ correlate with the error at \pv{rb.eoned.across} and
\pv{rb.xthree.across} (on the evaluation set, \pv{ra.eoned.rho} and \pv{ra.xthree.rho}).
Both rank which problems are hard; neither ranks the answers to one problem.

\section{Propagation of measurement noise: structure is not forecast quality}
\label{sec:act3}

% FINDINGS #17, #23 (X1: no A6c propagation), #27 (stage B, D104, 3 seeds; results/stage_b/results.json), #28 (stage
% C, D106), #29 (stage D, D109); R4-1 (the training data never contained the behaviour); stage_b_dpos2 comparators.
\paragraph{Routes that did not propagate.}
Three training routes failed before the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:dpos}.
Supervised fine-tuning on $\sigstar$ itself (stage B, three seeds) taught the model to read the stated percentages,
not to propagate them: its A6c slope is \pv{rc.stageb.slope}, and its expected score is below RSS
(\pv{rc.stageb.rss}).
$\sigma$-only RL with the predictive reward (stage C) learned to copy the smallest stated percentage, and an explicit
propagation line trained on data without value-dependent derivatives (stage D) learned exponent templates rather than
derivatives; both scored below RSS and X1 in their single-seed pilots.
None of these training sets contained the behaviour.
The text model X1 does not propagate either: its A6c slope on the frozen set is \pv{rc.xone.slope}.

% D113 (the R4-1 arms), D118 / D125 (R4-3: K4, K5, K8), FINDINGS #30, #32; results/r5_diag/depth_ts.json (B-v2's
% Theil-Sen slopes, #34); Table confirmatory.
\paragraph{Implicit versus explicit.}
Both arms train on the same mixed-level problems; only D-pos-v2 writes its sensitivities (Section~\ref{sec:dpos}).
On the frozen set, the implicit arm B-v2 learns where the noise is but not how much: its A6c slope is \pv{rc.k4} (K4),
short of C2's practical threshold of \pv{stat.practical}, and its expected score is below RSS (K8, \pv{rc.k8}).
The explicit arm's expected score is above B-v2's (K5, \pv{rc.k5}, Holm-rejected).
Per depth, B-v2's Theil--Sen slopes (\pv{rc.btwo.ts}) lie well below its OLS slopes (\pv{rc.btwo.ols}): a few pairs
carry the latter.

% D118, D125 (P = K1 and C2 and C3), FINDINGS #32; results/stage_r4/confirmatory/results.json and stage_b_dpos2/
% (oracle-centred description per seed); Table confirmatory; Figure headline C.
\paragraph{The confirmatory verdict.}
The primary hypothesis P is \pv{rc.p.confirmed} (Table~\ref{tab:confirmatory}).
D-pos-v2's answer $\sigma$ has an A6c slope of \pv{rc.k1} (K1, Holm-rejected; per seed \pv{rc.kone.seeds}), above C2's
\pv{stat.practical}, and an A6b slope on computation-correct pairs of \pv{rc.cthree}, inside $\pm\pv{stat.margin}$
(C3).
All three of its $\sigma$ values propagate: the line's $X$ (b) at \pv{rc.k2} and the exact value from the line's own
terms (c) at \pv{rc.k3}.
Problem by problem, the stated $\sigma$ is close to $\sigstar$.
Per seed, the median $|\ln(\sigma/\sigstar)|$ is \pv{rc.mal.seeds} (RSS \pv{rc.mal.rss}, X1 \pv{rc.mal.xone}), the
slope of $\ln\sigma$ on $\ln\sigstar$ is \pv{rc.beta.seeds} (RSS \pv{rc.beta.rss}, X1 \pv{rc.beta.xone}), and $1\sigma$
coverage is \pv{rc.cov.seeds} (\pv{rc.cov.star} for $\sigstar$ itself on the same problems).

% D118 sec. 12 (the secondaries with equal prominence), D133 ("not rejected" is not equivalence; never "ties"),
% FINDINGS #32 (K6, K7, the gap); results/r5_diag/tail.json (rss_to_star_gap).
\paragraph{The score secondaries, with equal prominence.}
Propagating noise does not buy a better expected score.
Against RSS, D-pos-v2's difference is \pv{rc.k6} (K6, one-sided $p = \pv{rc.k6.p}$): not rejected, with per-seed
estimates of both signs (\pv{rc.ksix.seeds}).
Failure to reject is not equivalence; the interval allows a small loss or a small gain.
Measured against the gap between RSS and $\sigstar$, D-pos-v2 closes \pv{rc.ksix.gap} of it.
Against X1 the estimate is negative, \pv{rc.k7} (K7, $p = \pv{rc.k7.p}$).
K7 tested whether D-pos-v2 beats X1, so we read the negative estimate descriptively only: X1 scores better.
The learned $\sigma$ follows $\sigstar$ more closely than either heuristic and yet is not shown to forecast better than
either; propagating the noise correctly and forecasting well are different properties.

% D118 (the pre-specified depth split, outside Holm), FINDINGS #32; D137 item 4 and FINDINGS #34 (per-depth slopes are
% fragile: report OLS and Theil-Sen); R5-8 (depth 5 is chain-length extrapolation); results/r5_diag/depth_ts.json,
% results/r5_diag/depth5.json.
\paragraph{Depth \pv{gen.heldoutdepth}, and fragile per-depth slopes.}
The pre-specified depth split, outside the Holm family, locates the loss.
At the trained depths, D-pos-v2 is ahead of RSS (\pv{rc.dfour.k6}) and not distinguishable from X1
(\pv{rc.dfour.k7}).
At the held-out depth \pv{gen.heldoutdepth} it is below both (\pv{rc.dfive.k6} and \pv{rc.dfive.k7}), while its A6c
slope there is \pv{rc.dfive.kone}.
Depth \pv{gen.heldoutdepth} is extrapolation in chain length, one more step from the same three families, not to new
physical laws.
Single-depth slopes are fragile, so we give OLS and Theil--Sen side by side: by depth, the seed-mean slopes are
\pv{rc.depth.1}, \pv{rc.depth.2}, \pv{rc.depth.3}, \pv{rc.depth.4} and \pv{rc.depth.5}, and the number of pairs moved
in the wrong direction rises from \pv{rc.rev.first} per seed at depth \pv{rc.depth.first} to \pv{rc.rev.last} of
\pv{rc.depth.pairs} at depth \pv{gen.heldoutdepth}.
For one and the same adapter, the depth-\pv{gen.heldoutdepth} OLS slope is \pv{rc.dfive.dev.ols} on the development
set and \pv{rc.dfive.frozen.ols} on the frozen set, with \pv{rc.dfive.dev.rev} and \pv{rc.dfive.frozen.rev} reversed
pairs; Theil--Sen gives \pv{rc.dfive.dev.ts} and \pv{rc.dfive.frozen.ts}.
A few high-leverage reversed pairs decide the OLS slope, so we claim neither that the model propagates nor that it
fails to propagate at a single depth.

% R5-2 (a descriptive autopsy), D133; FINDINGS #35; results/r5_diag/tail.json; Table tail.
\paragraph{Where the loss is: the tail.}
The regret against $\sigstar$ is concentrated: the worst \pv{rc.tail.worst} of problems hold \pv{rc.tail.share} of
each seed's expected-score regret (Table~\ref{tab:tail}).
Their most common primary cause is a wrong value-dependent sensitivity: a wrong formula for the derivative
(\pv{rc.tail.formula}), or the right formula with the model's own wrong intermediates substituted
(\pv{rc.tail.subst}).
Decimal shifts in copying $X$ into the answer (\pv{rc.tail.copy}) and the wrong chain (\pv{rc.tail.chain}) follow.
The substitution errors tie the two error streams together: one wrong intermediate corrupts both $\mu$ and its
sensitivity.
Trimming the worst problems does not change the order of X1, D-pos-v2 and RSS.

% R5-3 (a diagnostic, never a method), D133; FINDINGS #35; results/r5_diag/tail.json; Table swaps.
\paragraph{Component swaps.}
Replacing one component of D-pos-v2's $\sigma$ at a time with its exact value shows which one costs the score
(Table~\ref{tab:swaps}).
With exact sensitivities, and the model's own levels, aggregation and copy, the $\sigma$ beats RSS
(\pv{rc.swap.s.rss}) and X1 (\pv{rc.swap.s.xone}), closes \pv{rc.swap.s.gap} of the RSS-to-$\sigstar$ gap, and its A6c
slope rises to \pv{rc.swap.s.slope}.
Exact levels (\pv{rc.swap.levels.gap} of the gap), exact aggregation (\pv{rc.swap.aggregation.gap}) or an exact copy
(\pv{rc.swap.copy.gap}) close little or none of it.
This counterfactual locates the loss; it is not a method, since no model here computes exact sensitivities.
The swaps' slopes use every pair, with an unanswered member at the floor, so D-pos-v2's own row reads
\pv{rc.swap.stated.slope} rather than K1's estimate on answered pairs.

% R5-4 (post-hoc exploratory; bounds chosen on the development sets and frozen first, D135); FINDINGS #36;
% results/r5_diag/fallback_confirmatory.json; Figure headline C.
\paragraph{A post-hoc guardrail: the RSS fallback.}
A fallback that replaces D-pos-v2's $\sigma$ by RSS whenever their ratio leaves \pv{rc.fb.bounds} (bounds chosen on
the development sets and fixed before this single application) fires on \pv{rc.fb.rate} of problems per seed.
It lifts the expected score above RSS (\pv{rc.fb.rss}) and X1 (\pv{rc.fb.xone}) and closes \pv{rc.fb.gap} of the gap,
but it erases most of the measured propagation: the A6c slope falls to \pv{rc.fb.slope}.
It is post-hoc and exploratory, it does not change K6 or K7, and a guardrail of this kind has to report both numbers.

% D113 sec. 7 / R4-4 (MC-A, a pre-registered diagnostic), FINDINGS #31; D119 / D122 (MC-B), FINDINGS #33, #37;
% results/mc_a/a6c_pairs/results.json, results/mc_b/a6c_pairs/results.json, results/r5_diag/cost.json.
\paragraph{Two black-box routes that failed: MC-A and MC-B.}
Executing the computation instead of predicting $\sigma$ did not help either (Section~\ref{sec:mc}).
MC-A, which re-asks B-v2 on $k = \pv{mca.k}$ perturbed copies, does not beat RSS: the robust spread's difference is
\pv{rc.mca.rss} (one-sided $p = \pv{rc.mca.p}$), so the pre-registered stopping rule fired and $k$ was not increased.
The model's own arithmetic errors swamp the input noise: the same perturbations pushed through the exact chain beat
RSS by \pv{rc.mca.oracle}, so the bound is attainable at this $k$ when the computation is exact.
The raw spread is a better correctness signal (Section~\ref{sec:act2}), not a better $\sigma$.
MC-B, in which the base model writes the problem's program, fails twice: \pv{rc.mcb.interface} of its programs fail at
the interface (no program, a wrong signature, or rejected), and of the programs that run, \pv{rc.mcb.semantic} compute
the wrong chain; \pv{rc.mcb.correct} are correct.
None of its pre-registered tests is rejected (\pv{rc.mcb.rejected}); against RSS its expected score differs by
\pv{rc.mcb.rss}.

\section{Generalization: Jacobians outside the training range, and a second model}
\label{sec:generalization}

% D147-D149 (R5-6 part J, pre-registered; 3 seeds per arm, frozen J set), FINDINGS #39; D147 sec. 10 (the clamped
% comparator; the subgroups are descriptive, outside Holm); D166 (R6-1: report the clamped comparator and the 43-pair
% caveat); D148 (part U not run, the freeze); results/r5_6/results.json; Tables j, jsub.
\paragraph{Fresh Jacobians.}
The J set keeps trained chains and magnitudes in range, but places one given's sensitivity outside every value that
chain had in training (Section~\ref{sec:pairs}).
P$_J$ is \pv{rd.p.confirmed} (Table~\ref{tab:j}): D-pos-v2's J-sensitivity slope is \pv{rd.jone} (J1), and its
J-invariance slope is \pv{rd.cthree} (C3), on \pv{rd.cthree.pairs} computation-correct pairs per seed.
The explicit arm's slope exceeds the implicit arm's by \pv{rd.jtwo} (J2).
Again there is no score gain: against RSS \pv{rd.jthree} (J3, \pv{rd.jthree.holm}) and against X1 \pv{rd.jfour} (J4,
\pv{rd.jfour.holm}).
The clamped comparator qualifies J1.
A propagator that is exact but clips every sensitivity into its chain's training range already reaches
\pv{rd.clamped}, so most of the set's contrast is reachable inside the training range.
D-pos-v2 exceeds it most where the dominant given was never dominant for that chain in training (\pv{rd.flip.dpos}
against \pv{rd.flip.clamped}; Table~\ref{tab:jsub}), but that subgroup rests on \pv{rd.flip.pairs} pairs and is
descriptive, outside the Holm family.
The claim is therefore narrow: propagation through Jacobians outside the training range of trained chains.
New formulas, compositions and physics were not tested.

% R5-5, D137 (the go rule), D139, D146 (the NO-GO accepted as pre-registered; item 1, the descriptive A6b slope);
% FINDINGS #38; results/stage_xm/pilot/results.json, results/stage_xm/go_dpos2/go.json; Table llama.
\paragraph{A second model family: an exploratory pilot that returned NO-GO.}
We repeated both recipes on \pv{model.llama} with one seed, on the frozen A6c and A6b sets, with R4-3's criterion as a
pre-registered go rule for running more seeds (Table~\ref{tab:llama}).
K1 and C2 replicate: D-pos-v2's A6c slope is \pv{rd.xm.slope} on \pv{rd.xm.pairs} pairs.
C3 cannot be measured, since only \pv{rd.xm.cthree.pairs} A6b pairs are computation-correct, so the go rule returned
\pv{rd.xm.go} and no further seeds were run.
We report this as a replication that failed at one seed, for that reason, with the same prominence a success would
have had.
Descriptively, outside the go rule, the A6b slope on all \pv{rd.xm.desc.pairs} answered pairs is \pv{rd.xm.desc}:
inside $\pm\pv{stat.margin}$ but excluding zero, so on this model the stated relative $\sigma$ shrinks slightly as the
target grows, where it should stay flat (\pv{model.base}'s single-seed estimate is \pv{rd.xm.qwen}).
We claim neither scale invariance nor any other cross-model result for \pv{model.llama}.
